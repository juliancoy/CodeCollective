AI Governance Overview: What’s Happening in International AI Competition?

**A free, 90-minute online workshop—no technical background needed**
AI is moving fast. The companies building it are moving fast. And the countries racing to lead in AI, especially the US and China, are moving fast too. Governments are trying to respond, but most people still don't have a clear picture of what is actually happening, or what good policy would even look like. This workshop gives you that bigger picture.
We'll start with a quick, plain-English overview of what AI is, how today's systems work at a high level, and why the last few years have felt so different from earlier AI progress. From there, we'll look at the industry forming around the most advanced AI: the major labs, the incentives they face, the race to build more capable models, and why that race, now as much between countries as between companies, has raised serious questions about safety and governance.
Then we'll turn to AI safety: what people mean by it, why it matters, and why it isn't just a technical issue for researchers in labs. AI already causes real problems today, from scams and misinformation to bias, privacy concerns, cyber risks, and job disruption. More advanced models could raise the stakes further, especially if they get better at persuasion, hacking, autonomous planning, scientific research, or acting on people's behalf in the real world.
Together, we'll explore questions like:

* **What is AI, really?** What makes today's systems different from older software, and why are people treating this moment as a major technological shift?
* **What is AI safety?** What are researchers, policymakers, and companies worried about, and which concerns are immediate versus longer-term?
* **Current risks:** How is AI already affecting scams, misinformation, privacy, jobs, education, and trust?
* **Near-future risks:** What could change if AI becomes significantly more capable over the next year or two?
* **International competition:** How are the US, China, and other countries competing for the lead in AI, through chips, export controls, and massive investment, and what does that race mean for safety?
* **AI legislation:** What laws and regulations have been passed so far, what are they trying to do, and how has the debate around them played out?
* **Governance options:** What policy tools are being discussed to handle more serious risks from advanced AI, and could rival countries ever agree on shared rules?

**What you'll get:**

* A beginner-friendly overview of AI as a technology and an industry
* A clear explanation of what AI safety means and why people disagree about it
* A grounded look at the risks AI poses today and the risks more advanced systems may pose soon
* A concrete overview of existing AI policy efforts and the debate around them
* A look at the global AI race: who the main players are, what's driving them, and why international cooperation is so hard
* A map of the main governance ideas being discussed, from transparency and safety testing to liability, licensing, compute governance, and international coordination

This workshop isn't about taking a simple "AI good" or "AI bad" position. The real world is messier than that.
AI could bring enormous benefits, and it could also create serious problems if we build and deploy it carelessly. The goal is to help you understand the policy conversation well enough to follow what's happening, evaluate proposals for yourself, and think clearly about what responsible governance might look like, at home and between nations, before the technology gets even more powerful.

What AI Can Do With Your Data: Privacy & Surveillance

**A free, 90-minute online workshop—no technical background needed**
Most of us already know that companies, governments, and apps collect a lot of data about us. That part isn't new.
**What *is* new is what AI makes possible with that data.**
For years, the privacy conversation has mostly been about tracking, ads, data brokers, social media, and surveillance. Those concerns still matter. But AI changes the picture: once powerful models can analyze huge amounts of text, images, location data, purchases, messages, and online behavior at scale, personal data becomes far more valuable, and far riskier.
This workshop looks at how data and privacy worked before AI, and what changes now that AI systems can search, summarize, infer, predict, and act on massive amounts of personal information.
Together, we'll explore questions like:

* **Surveillance:** How could AI make it easier to monitor people, spot patterns, and draw conclusions from huge amounts of data?
* **Incentives:** Why might companies and governments have stronger reasons than ever to collect, store, and analyze personal information?
* **Advertising and persuasion:** How could AI make targeted ads, political messaging, and behavioral manipulation more personalized and more effective?

**What you'll get:**

* A clear, beginner-friendly overview of how data privacy worked before modern AI
* A practical explanation of why AI changes the value and the danger of personal data
* Real examples of how AI is already being used in surveillance, advertising, and persuasion
* A framework for thinking clearly about privacy, power, and trust in a world where data is easier than ever to analyze

This isn't a workshop about deleting every app or becoming paranoid about technology. It's about understanding the new reality we're entering.
AI doesn't just make data more useful. It makes data more actionable. That means the privacy choices we make, as individuals, companies, and societies, are going to matter a lot more than they used to.
