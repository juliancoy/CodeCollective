Emerging Research in Cislunar Space and How to Get Involved

This workshop will introduce NASA’s upcoming Artemis missions, emerging research areas in cislunar space, and opportunities for programmers, engineers, students, and space enthusiasts to contribute to humanity’s return to the Moon. Participants will learn the fundamentals of orbital motion, reference frames, trajectory design, and orbit propagation before exploring current research in cislunar space situational awareness, lunar landing and descent, and space robotics. The session will conclude with a short Python demonstration of selenocentric orbit propagation using planetary ephemerides.
The workshop will be led by Scott Blender, a third-year Ph.D. student in aerospace engineering at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) and a researcher in the Advanced Space Concepts Laboratory. His research focuses on spacecraft guidance, cislunar space domain awareness, and lunar-surface sensor networks. He previously interned at AstronetX, a startup developing an optical system for cislunar space situational awareness.

The workshop will be organized as follows:

1. NASA’s Return to the Moon Through Artemis
2. Fundamentals of Orbital Motion: Kepler’s Laws and Newtonian Gravitation
3. Two-Body Orbit Propagation, Reference Frames, and Selenocentric Orbits
4. Emerging Cislunar Research: Space Situational Awareness, Lunar Landing and Descent, and Space Robotics
5. Live Python Demonstration: Ephemeris-Supported Orbit Propagation
6. Q&A and Opportunities to Get Involved

## Claude Code vs. Codex: Loops, Graphs, and Harnesses

This is a casual pair-programming meetup for developers experimenting with Claude Code, Codex, and agentic coding workflows.
There is no formal presentation and no fixed curriculum. Bring a laptop, a repository, a side project, or a workflow you want to test. We will work in small groups, compare approaches, debug problems, and share what is working.

Possible areas to explore include:

* Building iterative coding-agent loops
* Comparing Claude Code and Codex on the same task
* Designing graph-based agent workflows
* Creating reusable coding harnesses
* Managing context across large repositories
* Running tests and validating agent-generated changes
* Using sandboxes, permissions, and approval steps
* Coordinating subagents or parallel tasks
* Measuring latency, token usage, cost, and completion quality
* Debugging failed or incomplete agent runs

You do not need to be an expert in either tool. This meetup is for people who want hands-on time to experiment, pair with other developers, and learn through building.

Often, the door to Griffith Hall is locked at 7PM. If you find the door locked, please contact the security guard (usually in the booth in the parking lot) and tell them that the monthly PLUG (Philadelphia Linux User Group) meeting in Griffith Hall is tonight. If there is no one in the booth you can call UCS's public safety number at 215.895.1117 and they will radio personnel to unlock the door. Our sponsor is Zhiwei Liu in the Chemistry department. Security knows this and will almost always open the door for you and have it unlocked so other participants can enter without issue.
