# Maryland Data Centers and Energy Map: Implementation Plan

## 1. Goal and non-negotiable rules

Build a public, source-traceable map that answers, for each Maryland data center:

- Where is it, who owns it, who operates it, and what is its development status?
- How much grid power and annual energy does it use?
- Does it have an on-site natural-gas plant or other generation?
- What emergency generators and UPS/battery systems does it have?
- How many people work there, and how many jobs were promised?
- What investment, tax benefit, grant, loan, public infrastructure, or budget direction supports it?
- Which power plants operate nearby, what is their capacity, and how much electricity did each produce?
- What do Maryland residents pay for electricity, statewide and by serving utility?

The following rules are mandatory:

1. `datacenters/data/infrastructure.json` is the canonical flat list for data centers and power plants. One physical campus or separately permitted operating facility is one object. Do not build a nested owner/campus/building hierarchy.
2. Use `null`, plus an entry in `not_disclosed_fields`, when a value is unavailable. Never convert “not found” into zero or “no.”
3. Every nontrivial populated field must be supported by one or more IDs in `source_ids`.
4. A permit application describes what was proposed; an issued permit describes what was authorized; neither proves that equipment was built or is operating. Preserve those distinctions in `status` and `notes`.
5. Never use backup-generator nameplate capacity as data-center load, annual energy consumption, or production.
6. Corporate sustainability claims, corporate-wide employment, and corporate-wide energy use must not be presented as facility-specific facts.
7. Prefer final government datasets over preliminary or early-release datasets. If only preliminary data exist, label the record and the UI visibly.
8. Do not silently estimate. Directly reported and estimated values require separate fields, methods, and confidence labels.

The first release already provides:

- A Projects hover/focus dropdown generated by `js/reusableComponents.js`, with this project first.
- A data-center and energy map at `/datacenters.html`.
- Eighteen flat Maryland records: the original hyperscale, government, commercial-colocation, and proposal records plus a PeeringDB/operator audit that added Cogent Elkridge, two Atlantech sites, and three AiNET sites. Tenants at an already-recorded carrier hotel are not double-counted as separate physical facilities.
- Plan, permit, financing, public-opposition, and sentiment fields on every record, with filters in the map UI.
- Explicit unknown values for energy use, PUE, UPS rating, employment, investment, tax benefits, and public funding where the reviewed permits do not disclose them.

- The EIA 2024 Maryland residential average of 17.86 cents/kWh.
- Three locally archived MDE PDFs with SHA-256 hashes and a source register for archived and remote-only material.

This is a defensible seed, not a claim that only two data centers exist in Maryland. The statewide inventory is the next workstream.

## 4. Canonical data-center schema

Every data-center object in `infrastructure.json` must carry the same type-specific keys, even when their values are `null`. Every infrastructure object also carries `id`, `record_type`, `name`, coordinates, and `technology_tags`.

### Identity and location

```text
id, record_type, name, operator, owner, campus, status,
year_built, year_built_status, year_built_basis,
development_status, permit_status, air_permit_status, legal_status,
status_tags, year_built_source_ids, status_source_ids,
plan_detail, permit_detail, financing_detail,
street_address, city, county, state, postal_code,
latitude, longitude, coordinate_method, coordinate_checked_date
```

- `id`: stable lowercase slug. Never recycle an ID.
- `year_built`: the documented structure year for an existing facility, or the earliest operating-generator year for an EIA plant. Use `null` for unbuilt proposals and genuinely unknown years; never substitute the year of an unrelated pre-existing parcel structure.
- `year_built_status`: one of `documented`, `not built`, or `unknown`. `year_built_basis` must explain what the year represents and any important limitation.
- `development_status`: a concise current lifecycle description supported by `status_source_ids`.
- `permit_status`, `air_permit_status`, and `legal_status`: keep land-use/building approvals, air decisions, and litigation or enacted legal restrictions distinct. Use `not researched` or `not identified in reviewed records` instead of inferring approval or absence.
- `status_tags`: short filterable labels such as `Operating`, `Not built`, `Air permit approved`, `Permits paused`, or `Legal restriction enacted`.
- `year_built_source_ids` and `status_source_ids`: source-register IDs that support these fields. Every entity must carry both arrays.
- Coordinates are derived data. Record the geocoder or parcel GIS used and the check date.

### Physical and utility profile

```text
building_count, site_acres, utility,
reported_power_capacity_mw, reported_power_capacity_basis,
reported_grid_demand_mw, reported_annual_energy_mwh, reported_pue,
power_scale_class, power_scale_label, power_scale_mw,
power_scale_value_kind, power_scale_detail, power_scale_tag,
power_scale_source_ids,
estimated_grid_demand_mw, estimated_annual_energy_mwh,
estimate_method, estimate_confidence
```

Add the four estimate fields before publishing any estimates. Keep reported and estimated numbers distinct in the UI.

Power-scale classifications use normal reported grid demand first. When demand is unavailable, a published facility, critical-power, protected-power, or utility-feed capacity may provide an explicitly labeled upper-envelope proxy. Never classify normal draw from backup-generator nameplate capacity.

```text
sub-megawatt: under 1 MW
small: 1 to under 5 MW
medium: 5 to under 20 MW
large: 20 to under 100 MW
very-large: 100 MW or more
unknown: neither normal demand nor facility power capacity is disclosed
```

### On-site power and resilience

```text
on_site_natural_gas_power_plant,
on_site_generation_capacity_mw, on_site_generation_technology,
backup_generation_fuel, backup_generator_count,
backup_generator_capacity_mw, backup_generator_detail,
ups_technology, ups_capacity_mw, ups_energy_mwh
```

`on_site_natural_gas_power_plant` is a text status, not a guessed Boolean. Allowed examples are `permitted`, `proposed`, `operating`, `not identified in reviewed records`, and `not yet researched`.

UPS power (`MW`) and stored energy (`MWh`) are different measurements. Do not derive one from the other without a sourced runtime assumption.

### Water, workforce, finance, and provenance

```text
cooling_water_detail,
employees_current, employees_committed,
capital_investment_usd, tax_incentive_detail, public_funding_detail,
public_opposition_status, public_sentiment_score, public_sentiment_label,
sentiment_basis, sentiment_source_ids, sentiment_assessed_date,
sentiment_confidence,
last_verified_date, source_ids, not_disclosed_fields, notes
```

Add separate numeric fields later for recurring tax abatements or appropriations only when the source supplies an actual amount and fiscal year. Do not parse an incentive program's maximum or eligibility threshold as an award.

### Public opposition and sentiment

The score measures the strength and direction of documented public evidence; it is not a representative poll and must never be described as one.

```text
-2  strong sustained site-specific opposition
-1  documented site-specific opposition
 0  mixed or materially conflicting evidence
+1  documented site-specific support
+2  strong sustained site-specific support
null insufficient reliable evidence
```

Use formal hearing records, enacted restrictions, petitions with visible counts, organizational letters, public surveys with disclosed methods, and attributable high-quality reporting. Do not score a long-operating facility negatively merely because its county later passed a general moratorium. Record that broader context in `sentiment_basis`, leave the score `null`, and use `insufficient evidence`. Reassess dated scores when a permit decision, election, referendum, settlement, cancellation, or new representative survey changes the record.

## 5. Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Issued permit, final order, recorded deed, adopted budget, executed agreement, audited filing, or final federal survey.
2. Filed application, site plan, environmental assessment, utility interconnection filing, tariff, or legislative fiscal note.
3. SEC filing, issuer annual report, IRS Form 990, bond official statement, or audited financial statement.
4. Named company announcement or investor presentation.
5. Reputable reporting, only as a lead or to document an attributed statement absent from primary records.
6. Commercial directories, crowdsourced maps, and vendor marketing, only as discovery leads. Never use them as the sole source for capacity, consumption, jobs, or public subsidy.

If two primary sources conflict, keep the newer applicable value and explain the conflict in `notes`. Do not average values.

## 6. Build the complete Maryland facility inventory

Perform these steps county by county. Start with Frederick, Montgomery, Prince George's, Anne Arundel, Baltimore County, Baltimore City, Howard, Harford, and Washington County, then cover the remaining counties.

1. Search the MDE data-center hub, air permits, generator permits, water-appropriation permits, discharge permits, brownfield records, and Environmental Service Center.
2. Search each county's planning commission agendas, site-plan archives, zoning cases, building permits, development pipeline, and GIS parcels for `data center`, `datacenter`, `server`, known operators, and project aliases.
3. Search Maryland PSC cases for large-load service, substations, transmission, CPCNs, co-located generation, and special contracts.
4. Search PJM interconnection queues and transmission planning documents. Treat the queue as a proposed grid project, not proof that a data center exists.
5. Search Maryland Commerce announcements and request the current qualified-data-center certifications and annual renewals under the Maryland Public Information Act if no public list exists.
6. Reconcile names and addresses. A landlord, campus developer, tenant/operator, and permit applicant may be different entities at one address.
7. Create one record per physical campus or separately permitted facility. Use `campus` and `notes` to explain relationships and avoid double counting landlord and tenant records.
8. Record cancelled and closed projects; keep them hidden by default rather than deleting history.
9. Require at least one primary source before publishing a map marker.

For each county, add an inventory checklist to a tracking issue or to a new `datacenters/data/inventory-status.json` with `county`, `searched_date`, `systems_searched`, `records_found`, and `reviewer`.

## 7. Profile actual energy use

Use this evidence order:

1. Utility bills, facility environmental reports, government disclosure reports, or metered load in a regulatory filing.
2. Utility interconnection/service agreement or PSC filing that clearly identifies the facility's expected peak demand.
3. Operator sustainability report with a facility-specific measurement and reporting period.
4. An explicit estimate derived from IT load, PUE, and utilization, with each input sourced.

For a permitted peak demand of `D MW`, do not calculate annual energy as `D × 8,760` and label it actual use. If an estimate is necessary, use:

```text
estimated_annual_energy_mwh = demand_mw × 8,760 × assumed_load_factor
```

Store the load factor, its source, calculation date, and uncertainty in `estimate_method`. Label the result `estimated`, not `reported`.

Watch the Maryland Data Center Impact Analysis required by HB 270 / Chapter 3. The final report is due September 1, 2026. On or after that date, search the Maryland General Assembly, Department of Legislative Services, MDE, and Maryland Energy Administration sites, archive the report if reasonably sized, and use it to refresh statewide load, emissions, water, and economic fields.

## 8. Identify on-site gas plants and backup systems

For each facility:

1. Read the MDE air permit equipment schedule. Separate emergency diesel generators, non-emergency generators, boilers, turbines, and combined heat and power equipment.
2. Search PSC CPCN dockets for generating stations. A utility substation is not an on-site power plant.
3. Search the EPA Facility Registry Service, ECHO, GHGRP, and eGRID by address and operator.
4. Search EIA-860 by plant address and owner. A matching generator record establishes an EIA-reporting power plant; it does not establish that all output serves the data center.
5. Sum generator ratings only when every unit and rating are enumerated. Put the arithmetic in `backup_generator_detail` or a reproducible extraction script.
6. Label fuel, generator role, permit status, and operational status separately.

Do not say “no gas plant” merely because an air permit discusses only diesel generators. Say `not identified in reviewed records` and list which records were reviewed.

## 9. Research UPS and battery systems

UPS details are frequently confidential. Use only:

- Electrical one-line diagrams filed with a planning or fire review.
- Fire-code/hazardous-material permits listing battery chemistry and energy.
- MDE hazardous-material or emergency-planning records.
- A facility-specific vendor case study that names the site and installed model.
- Procurement, bond, or audited project documents.

Capture chemistry, manufacturer/model, count, rated power, rated energy, runtime, topology, and document date as separate fields if they become available. If a document gives “15 minutes at full load,” retain that statement; do not convert it to MWh unless the referenced full load is also known.

## 10. Research personnel and economic claims

Maintain separate values for:

- `employees_current`: verified people currently employed at the site.
- `employees_committed`: jobs promised in an incentive agreement or application.
- Temporary construction jobs, which need a future separate field and must not be merged with permanent employment.

Search executed incentive agreements, Commerce certifications, county economic-development agreements, planning staff reports, WARN notices, operator job pages, and state labor data. A Maryland qualified-data-center applicant generally must satisfy program job rules, but the program minimum is not the facility's committed or current employment unless a facility-specific certification says so.

Use 10-K filings as follows:

1. Resolve the public parent company's CIK.
2. Fetch the latest filing and relevant exhibits directly from SEC EDGAR.
3. Search for the facility name, address, Maryland, capital commitments, leases, power purchase obligations, environmental contingencies, and headcount.
4. Use a corporate total only in a corporate field; never copy it into `employees_current`.
5. Record accession number, filing date, page/section, and exact field supported in `sources.json`.

Use Form 990 only for nonprofits, public authorities, or tax-exempt counterparties actually connected to a project. Pull machine-readable XML from IRS TEOS, inspect Parts VIII–X, Schedule I (grants), Schedule J (compensation), Schedule R (related entities), and Schedule O. Do not assume a for-profit operator has a Form 990.

## 11. Track public money and official budget direction

Search all of the following for each operator, owner, project name, address, parcel, and known subsidiary:

- Maryland operating and capital budgets, budget books, supplemental budgets, and agency budget detail.
- Maryland Board of Public Works agendas/minutes and capital-grant records.
- Maryland Commerce, Maryland Energy Administration, MEDCO, and local economic-development grants, loans, bonds, and tax credits.
- County and municipal adopted budgets, capital-improvement programs, tax-increment districts, PILOT agreements, road/water/sewer projects, and bond official statements.
- Maryland tax-expenditure reports and the Data Center Maryland sales/use-tax exemption program.
- PSC rate cases, large-load tariffs, special contracts, transmission upgrades, and utility cost-allocation testimony.
- USAspending awards and subawards; SAM.gov assistance listings and contracts; Grants.gov notices; EDA, DOE, NTIA, and EPA awards.
- Federal agency congressional budget justifications when an appropriation directs a named regional computing or grid project.

For every match, distinguish:

```text
authorization -> appropriation -> obligation/award -> outlay/payment
```

Do not call an authorization or requested budget an expenditure. Store amount, fiscal year, stage, awarding agency, recipient legal entity, award/agreement number, purpose, and source ID.

## 12. Power plants and production refresh

The generator script expects final annual EIA workbooks. To reproduce the current data:

```bash
mkdir -p /tmp/eia860-2024 /tmp/eia923-2024
curl -L https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia860/xls/eia8602024.zip -o /tmp/eia860-2024.zip
curl -L https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia923/archive/xls/f923_2024.zip -o /tmp/eia923-2024.zip
unzip -o /tmp/eia860-2024.zip -d /tmp/eia860-2024
unzip -o /tmp/eia923-2024.zip -d /tmp/eia923-2024
python3 datacenters/generate_datacenter_energy_data.py \
  --plant-workbook /tmp/eia860-2024/2___Plant_Y2024.xlsx \
  --generator-workbook /tmp/eia860-2024/3_1_Generator_Y2024.xlsx \
  --generation-workbook /tmp/eia923-2024/EIA923_Schedules_2_3_4_5_M_12_2024_Final.xlsx \
  --year 2024 --state MD \
  --output datacenters/data/infrastructure.json
```

Install `pandas` and `openpyxl` if the environment lacks them. Upgrade to a newer year only after final EIA-860 and EIA-923 files both exist. Do not mix an early-release capacity file with final generation without a visible version note.

The current transform includes operable EIA-860 plants and EIA-923 generation/fuel rows. Add an explicit storage-sheet join before claiming statewide generation totals, because storage is reported on a separate EIA-923 worksheet. The UI currently calls its total “plant generation,” not total statewide supply.

## 13. Residential electricity costs

Keep the statewide EIA annual average, then add utility-level records for BGE, Potomac Edison, Pepco, Delmarva Power, SMECO, and municipal/cooperative providers.

For each utility and effective period, capture:

- EIA annual residential average price, sales, revenue, and customers.
- PSC-approved tariff effective date and case number.
- Customer charge, volumetric supply rate, distribution rate, riders/surcharges, taxes, and any time-of-use periods.
- Standard-offer-service price separately from total delivered price.
- A reproducible sample bill at 500, 1,000, and 1,500 kWh/month.

Never compare a data center's negotiated large-load tariff directly with the residential statewide average without labeling the different customer classes and cost components.

## 14. Download and archive policy

1. Put PDFs, DOCX files, scanned permits, and other non-machine-readable primary records in `datacenters/downloads/` when each file is under 20 MB.
2. For files 20 MB or larger, retain the official URL, retrieved date, approximate size, and a download note in `sources.json`; do not commit the file.
3. Name local documents `YYYY-publisher-project-document-type.ext`.
4. Compute `sha256sum` after download and store it as `local_sha256`.
5. Keep the original file unchanged. Put extracted text in a future `datacenters/extracted/` directory only if it is used by a reproducible parser or review process.
6. If a PDF is scanned, run OCR on a copy. Mark OCR output as derived and spot-check all numbers against the page image.
7. Never archive a commercial article or copyrighted report merely for convenience; store its URL and a short factual note.

## 15. Source-register procedure

Add one object to `sources.json` before adding facts from a new source. Required fields are:

```text
id, title, publisher, document_date, retrieved_date, url,
local_path, local_sha256, source_tier, used_for
```

Optional fields are `storage_note`, `accession_number`, `docket_number`, `case_number`, `pages`, and `supersedes_source_id`.

Use stable IDs. If a document changes at the same URL, download and hash the new copy, create a new source ID with its date, and update only the records it actually supersedes.

## 16. UI work after the inventory grows

Implement in this order:

1. Add filters for county, status, operator, power-plant technology, and source completeness.
2. Add marker clustering only when the map becomes slow; do not add it preemptively.
3. Add a “reported / estimated / unknown” badge beside energy, UPS, jobs, and public-money fields.
4. Add a timeline per facility from permit/application dates without changing the canonical flat facility list. Timeline events should live in a separate flat `events.json` keyed by `datacenter_id`.
5. Add distance-to-plant display as a derived UI calculation. Do not imply that the nearest plant supplies the facility.
6. Add a source-detail drawer with document page references and archived-copy hashes.
7. Keep keyboard operation: Projects opens on hover and focus, Escape closes it, map results are buttons, and every map fact is also available in the text panel.

## 17. Automated validation

Add `tests/test_datacenters.py` with these assertions:

1. All four JSON files parse.
2. All top-level values are arrays.
3. IDs are unique within each file.
4. Every data-center object has the complete canonical key set.
5. Every `source_id` resolves to `sources.json`.
6. Every local source exists and its SHA-256 matches.
7. Latitude and longitude fall within Maryland's practical bounding box.
8. Numeric capacities, generation, employment, investment, and rates are nonnegative, except net generation where negative storage values may be legitimate.
9. `backup_generator_capacity_mw` is not copied into `reported_grid_demand_mw` without a distinct supporting source.
10. The Projects dropdown contains exactly the projects displayed on `projects.html`, with Data Centers & Energy first.

Add a browser smoke test that serves the repository over HTTP and verifies:

- `/datacenters.html` loads without console errors.
- Leaflet creates the map.
- Both data-center markers and the power-plant layer render.
- Search filters the text list.
- Selecting a marker exposes sources and unknown fields.
- The Projects menu works with hover, keyboard focus, Escape, and a touch-sized viewport.

## 18. Manual acceptance checklist

Before publishing any refresh:

1. Run JSON and JavaScript syntax checks.
2. Run the data-center validation tests and browser smoke test.
3. Serve the site locally; do not test with `file://`, because JSON fetches will fail.
4. Compare counts displayed by the page with JSON counts.
5. Inspect Aligned and Amazon marker locations against the official address/parcel map.
6. Open every locally archived PDF and every remote official link.
7. Confirm the source panel never labels an estimate as reported.
8. Confirm emergency-generator capacity is described as backup capacity everywhere.
9. Check desktop hover, keyboard navigation, narrow mobile layout, and screen-reader labels.
10. Build through `STRICT_TS=1 VITE_PIDP_BASE_URL=https://id.codecollective.us ./scripts/build_cloudflare_site.sh` and confirm the build output contains the HTML, JSON, JavaScript, CSS, and PDFs.
11. After deployment, verify `/datacenters.html`, all four JSON endpoints, and one archived PDF return HTTP 200 from `https://codecollective.us`.

## 19. Definition of done for the statewide version

The statewide version is complete only when:

- Every Maryland county has a dated inventory search record.
- Every published facility has a primary source and an explicit operational status.
- Energy, on-site generation, UPS, personnel, investment, incentives, and public funding are either sourced or visibly unknown for every facility.
- Power-plant and residential-rate data are reproducible from final EIA sources.
- Facility-specific PSC, MDE, planning, SEC/IRS, and budget searches have been performed and logged.
- The September 1, 2026 Maryland impact report has been reviewed once available.
- Automated and manual acceptance checks pass.

Do not call the project complete merely because the map has many markers. Completeness means each county and each required field has been searched, sourced, and honestly classified.
